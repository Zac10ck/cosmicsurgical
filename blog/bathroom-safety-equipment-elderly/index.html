Bathroom safety equipment for the elderly

The bathroom is where most elderly falls happen — wet floor, nothing to hold, a low seat to rise from. Four items fix most of it.

SMBy Saji V MathewManaging Director, Cosmic Surgicals · 16 September 2026 · 4 min read

Ask any orthopaedic ward in Kerala where their elderly hip fractures happened and the answer is the same: the bathroom. Wet floor, nothing to hold, a low toilet to rise from, and often the door locked from inside so help arrives late.

Four items fix most of it, and they cost less than one day in hospital.

1. Shower chair

The most important item. It moves bathing from standing to sitting, which removes the riskiest thing a frail person does every day.

  • Fixed shower stool or chair — plastic seat, rust-resistant frame, rubber-tipped legs, adjustable height. Simple and sufficient for someone who can walk in.
  • Shower chair with back and arms — for anyone who tires or needs support to rise
  • Shower commode chair — wheeled and water-resistant, with a commode opening. The person is wheeled in, uses the toilet by positioning over it, and is showered in the same chair. For those who cannot walk to the bathroom, this is the answer. See our commode guide.

Check the weight rating, that the feet are non-slip, and that the seat has drainage holes.

2. Grab bars

Fitted where the person naturally reaches for support: beside the toilet, at the shower entrance, beside the shower chair, and at the wash basin.

  • Stainless steel or coated steel, textured grip
  • Anchored through the tile into solid masonry — never glued, never suction
  • Angled or horizontal depending on the movement; a fitter or physiotherapist can advise
  • Do not rely on towel rails, basin edges or the door frame — they are not load-bearing

3. Raised toilet seat

Many Kerala homes have low Western toilets, and some elderly people still use Indian-style ones. Both need a deep squat that ageing knees and hips cannot manage safely, and the rise from a low seat is where falls happen.

  • Raised seat that clips onto the existing toilet — adds 10–15 cm
  • Raised seat with arms — gives something to push up from; strongly preferred
  • Toilet surround frame — free-standing arms around the toilet where fixing a seat is not possible
  • Commode chair over an Indian-style toilet — converts it to a seated toilet without construction

4. Non-slip flooring

  • Non-slip mats inside the shower area and on the floor outside it, with suction or rubber backing that actually grips
  • Rubber-soled bathroom footwear, not smooth chappals
  • Dry the floor after each use; keep a squeegee or a dedicated floor cloth
  • Consider anti-slip treatment for very smooth tiles

Beyond the four

  • Hand-held shower — the person washes seated without turning under a fixed head
  • Long-handled sponge and washing aids for reaching feet and back
  • Door that opens outward or can be unlocked from outside — so help can reach a fallen person
  • Bell or alarm within reach of the shower chair and toilet
  • Good lighting, and a night light on the route
  • Remove the threshold step into the bathroom, or mark it clearly and add a rail

Order of work

  1. Shower chair and non-slip mat — same day, no installation
  2. Grab bars beside toilet and shower — needs a fitter
  3. Raised toilet seat with arms
  4. Hand-held shower, lighting, door lock
