Adult diapers and underpads: getting the basics right

Almost every leak we hear about is a sizing problem, not an absorbency problem. Buying a higher absorbency in the wrong size makes it worse, not better.

SMBy Saji V MathewManaging Director, Cosmic Surgicals · 9 September 2026 · 4 min read

Continence supplies are the quiet, recurring cost of home care, and the category families most often buy badly — usually by reacting to a leak with a higher absorbency rating when the actual problem was fit.

Sizing decides everything

Leakage almost always happens at the leg openings, not through the pad. That means:

  • Too large → gaping at the legs → leaks, regardless of how absorbent the product is
  • Too small → pressure marks, discomfort, skin damage at the waist and thighs

Measure the waist and the hip circumference and match against that brand’s chart. Sizes are not consistent across manufacturers — a “large” in one brand is a medium in another. When switching brands, re-check the measurement rather than assuming.

A correct fit sits snug against the inner thigh with no gap, and the waist tapes or elastic hold without digging in.

Tape-style vs pull-up

Tape-style (adhesive tabs)

Opens flat. Can be changed with the patient lying down, without removing lower clothing or requiring them to stand. This is the right choice for bedridden patients and for anyone a caregiver changes.

Pull-up style

Worn like underwear. Suits patients who are mobile and can manage part of the routine themselves. Where the patient can still use a pull-up, use one — the independence and dignity it preserves are worth more than the small price difference.

Absorbency: match it to the interval, not the anxiety

Higher absorbency means a bulkier product, more heat and more cost. Choose the level that comfortably covers the actual interval between changes — typically a lighter product for daytime with regular changes, and a higher-absorbency product overnight.

Buying maximum absorbency for every change is a common and expensive habit, and in a humid climate the extra bulk works against skin health.

Underpads earn their keep

Disposable or washable underpads placed on the bed protect the mattress and, more usefully, mean an accident requires changing a pad rather than stripping and remaking the entire bed at 2 AM.

Where an air mattress is in use, keep bedding layers minimal — a single underpad, laid smooth. Thick layering defeats the pressure redistribution the mattress is there to provide.

Skin care, particularly in Kerala’s humidity

Moisture against skin is the main driver of both irritation and pressure sores, and the local climate does not help.

  • Change promptly whenever soiled — this is the single most important measure
  • Clean gently with water or a no-rinse cleanser; avoid vigorous rubbing
  • Dry completely before applying a fresh product, including skin folds
  • Use a barrier cream on skin regularly exposed to moisture
  • Allow air time where the situation permits — even short periods help
  • Inspect daily for redness, particularly at the sacrum, buttocks and inner thighs

Redness that does not fade within a few minutes of pressure being relieved should be reported to the treating clinician. See our bed sore prevention guide for the wider routine.

Managing the cost

Over months of care, consumables outweigh most equipment purchases. Two practical points:

  • Confirm the size before bulk-buying. A carton in the wrong size is money gone.
  • Then buy in quantity. Once size and brand are settled, wholesale rates make a real difference.

Buy one pack, confirm the fit over a few days, then order the carton.
