Water bed or air bed: which is right for an elderly patient?

Families in Kerala ask us this almost daily. Both work. The right answer depends on the patient's weight, how much they move, and who will look after the equipment.

SMBy Saji V MathewManaging Director, Cosmic Surgicals · 16 September 2026 · 4 min read

When an elderly parent becomes bedridden, the first equipment question most families in Kerala ask is not about the bed frame — it is “water bed or air bed?” Both are pressure-relieving mattresses. Both prevent bed sores. They do it in different ways, and they suit different households.

How each one prevents bed sores

Air bed (alternating pressure mattress)

A mattress of air cells connected to an electric pump. The pump inflates one set of cells while deflating the other, then reverses — usually every 10 to 15 minutes. No part of the skin carries weight for long, so blood flow is restored before tissue is damaged. It works even when the patient cannot move at all.

Water bed

A mattress filled with water. The patient floats on a surface that spreads their weight evenly across the whole body, so there are no high-pressure points at the sacrum, hips or heels. It is passive — there is no pump and nothing cycling — and it relies on the patient shifting slightly now and then.

Choose an air bed when…

  • The patient is fully bedridden and cannot reposition at all
  • A pressure sore already exists or has occurred before
  • The patient is heavier — tubular air beds carry higher weight ratings
  • The mattress may need to be moved between rooms or homes
  • Power supply is reliable, or there is an inverter

Choose a water bed when…

  • The patient still turns a little on their own
  • Power cuts are frequent and there is no inverter
  • The patient is disturbed by any noise at night
  • The bed will stay in one place for a long time
  • The floor and frame can take the weight — over 100 kg when filled

Three mistakes we see often

  1. Buying a bubble air bed for a fully immobile, heavy patient. Bubble types are for moderate risk. Fully bedridden patients need the tubular type.
  2. Piling sheets and blankets on top. Thick layers defeat the pressure relief of either mattress. One fitted sheet, pulled smooth.
  3. Treating the mattress as the whole answer. Neither replaces repositioning, skin checks and keeping the skin dry. Read our bed-sore prevention guide for the full routine.
